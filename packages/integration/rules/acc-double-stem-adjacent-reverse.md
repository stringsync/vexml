### The rule

When double-stemmed adjacent or overlapping notes place the higher-pitched note to the left of the lower-pitched note, the accidental order may be reversed so each accidental sits closer to its own note. See Behind Bars p. 91.

### The test case

`accidentals-voices.musicxml` isolates each case in its own measure.

- Measure 6. Two double-stemmed seconds with voice 1 (higher) on the left and voice 2 (lower) on the right: B♭4/A♮4 and G#4/F#4.

### Renderers

- Behind Bars: agrees, states the accidental order may be reversed to mirror the left-to-right note arrangement
- MuseScore: differs, keeps conventional order (higher accidental closer, lower to the left) on both seconds
- Verovio: differs, keeps conventional order (higher accidental closer, lower to the left) on both seconds
- OSMD: differs, keeps conventional order (higher accidental closer, lower to the left) on both seconds

### Why a person decides

Behind Bars states that the accidental order may be reversed when the higher note sits left, leaving the choice between conventional order and reversed order open.

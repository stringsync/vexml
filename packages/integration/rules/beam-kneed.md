### The rule

A beam centred between opposite-direction stems (a kneed beam) must keep every stem at least 2½ stave-spaces long, keep the top and bottom outer stave lines clear of the beam, and slant in the direction of the outer pitch interval (Behind Bars p. 26). Slanting with the interval makes a mid-stave beam stand out cleanly from the stave lines.

### The test case

`beams.musicxml`: measure 23 holds two kneed eighth-note pairs, A5 (down-stem) to C4 (up-stem) and A5 (down-stem) to A3 (up-stem), measuring every stem length, checking that neither beam touches the top or bottom stave line, and checking that both beams fall with the descending interval.

### What to look for

In measure 23, blue reference lines mark the outer stave lines and green brackets confirm all four stems stay at least `2½` spaces with outer lines clear, while red boxes on both beams report `rises` where the descending intervals require falling beams (`falls`).

# dot-unison-one-voice

### The rule

A dotted and undotted unison may share a head when the rhythms are obvious. Separate the heads when needed for clarity. The upper voice usually goes first, and putting the undotted head first is also permitted. See Behind Bars p. 59.

### The test case

`duration-dots.musicxml` uses one attack per measure to isolate the dot placement.

- Measure 27. Dotted and plain notes of the same head type in unison.
- Measure 28. Dotted and plain notes with distinct head types in unison.

### Checks

1. Is it clear which of the two rhythms takes the dot?
   Crop: measure 27, 7 spaces, 3 above, 3 below
2. Does the separated layout identify which head is dotted?
   Crop: measure 28, 7 spaces, 3 above, 3 below

### Why a person decides

Whether the rhythm is clear depends on the passage. The book offers more than one acceptable ordering of separate heads.

### The rule

When two parts cross in pitch, place the down-stem part on the left and the up-stem part slightly to its right, so the heads of one part never sit on the heads of the other and neither stem seems to hold both parts' notes. The other stave aligns with the left part. A lone crossed second may let its heads touch; crossed chords stand visibly apart. (Behind Bars p. 53)

### The test case

`double-stem-chords.musicxml` crosses A4 and B4 in measure 11 and two chords in measure 12, each against an independent reference onset.

### Checks

1. Is the down-stem head slightly left of the up-stem head?
   Crop: measure 11, 7 spaces

2. Are the stems visibly separate with the accompaniment aligned to the left voice?
   Crop: measure 11, part 1, 7 spaces, 4 above, 12 below

3. Do the crossed chords stand slightly apart, with no head of one part over a head of the other?
   Crop: measure 12, part 1, 7 spaces, 4 above, 12 below
